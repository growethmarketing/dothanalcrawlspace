Foundation Repair

Most Dothan Foundation Problems Start in the Crawl Space

Dothan's clay soil swells when it's wet and shrinks when it dries out. Every season, that movement pushes and pulls on the piers and walls holding your home up. Add years of moisture under the house, and you get the problems homeowners call us about: floors that slope or sag, doors that stick, and cracks in block walls.

Because most homes here are built on crawl spaces, we can usually fix the problem from underneath — and fix the moisture that caused it, so it doesn't come back.

Foundation Repairs We Handle

  • Sagging floor support — adjustable steel support posts added under weak beams to level and hold the floor
  • Joist and beam repair — rotted or cracked joists sistered or replaced before the damage spreads
  • Pier and footing problems — sinking or leaning piers shimmed, reset or supplemented
  • Foundation crack sealing — cracks in block or poured walls sealed to stop water and pests
  • Drainage and water control — interior drains and sump pumps that take pressure off foundation walls

Water coming in through the walls? See foundation & basement waterproofing. Rot in the floor framing? See crawl space repair.

Signs of Foundation Trouble

  • Floors that slope, sag, or feel bouncy
  • Doors and windows that stick or won't latch
  • Cracks in drywall above doors and windows
  • Stair-step cracks in block foundation walls
  • Gaps between baseboards and the floor
  • Piers in the crawl space that lean or have sunk

When We Bring in an Engineer

Most of what we see is fixable from the crawl space. If we find signs of major structural movement — walls bowing in, large or fast-growing cracks — we tell you before any work begins and recommend a structural engineer's assessment.

Foundation Repair FAQ

Is a sagging floor a foundation problem?
Usually not. In Dothan, most sagging floors come from moisture-damaged joists or sinking support piers in the crawl space. Both can be fixed from underneath the house without major foundation work.
Are cracks in my foundation walls serious?
Thin hairline cracks are common and often only need sealing. Cracks wider than about 1/4 inch, stair-step cracks that keep growing, or walls that bow inward should be looked at quickly.
Do you work with structural engineers?
Yes. If we find signs of major structural movement, we tell you before any work begins and recommend getting a structural engineer's assessment.
